The rule we enforce

A treaty limit expressed in days, tracked day by day.

Where a cross-border employee performs their work decides which country may tax their salary and collect social security. Once the days spent working from home pass the limit, the obligation shifts. Remote Days keeps a live count against that limit for every employee.

Why it is hard

A spreadsheet cannot hold a compliance position together.

The obligation is triggered by a running count nobody owns, on data nobody quite trusts, until it is too late to unwind.

The count is invisible until it is not

Nobody notices day 35 until payroll, a tax office or an inspection does. By then the whole year is already exposed, not just the day.

Manual tracking fails quietly

Missed rows, duplicated weeks, forgotten holidays and inconsistent definitions of "a day worked from home" — none of it surfaces until someone audits the file.

Evidence is the part that sinks you

A spreadsheet cell proves nothing. Inspectors ask who declared what, on which date, and who changed it afterwards — and they ask long after the file has been overwritten.

How it works

Three steps, and the count takes care of itself.

Most teams are running inside a day — the work is importing people and deciding your policy, not configuring software.

Bring your people in

Add employees one by one or import a CSV. Set each person's country of residence and work country — that pair decides which threshold applies. Roles decide what they can see.

They declare where they are

One click from the daily email, the web app or the mobile app. Weekends and public holidays are skipped, and every declaration is timestamped and attributed.

You monitor and prove

Days used, days remaining and a status band per employee. When an auditor asks, you export the record instead of rebuilding it.

Corrections with an approval trail

People forget, and the fix should not be a silent edit. An employee raises a request with a reason; HR approves or rejects it. The requested change, the reason, and the decision all stay attached to the record.

An activity trail you can hand over

When the question arrives — usually long after the fact — nobody has to remember anything. Every create, update and override is recorded with who did it, to which record, why, and what changed.

  • Timestamped entries for creates, updates and overrides
  • The actor, the target record, and the reason given
  • Filter by date range and export the period an auditor names
app.remotedays.app/admin/countries

Thresholds you control, not the vendor

Treaties and administrative positions move. The threshold per country is a setting in the admin console, so a change in the rule is a two-minute edit rather than a support ticket and a release.

Security & compliance

Built for a system of record, not for a demo.

This is attendance and location data about named people. It is handled accordingly, and the controls are the ordinary ones you would expect to find in a review.

Sessions in httpOnly cookies Tokens are never exposed to page scripts, and are set with same-site protections.
Passwords hashed with bcrypt Credentials are stored as adaptive hashes, never in a recoverable form.
Rate limiting and lockout Sign-in and password-reset endpoints are throttled, and repeated failures are tracked per address.
Parameterised queries and strict CORS No string-built SQL, an allowlist of origins, and hardened HTTP security headers.
Every change is attributable Entry mutations record the actor, the reason and the previous value.
EU data residency Personal data is stored on servers in the European Union, with GDPR-ready processing.

Single sign-on with your directory

Connect Microsoft Entra ID over OpenID Connect and your people sign in with their work account. Sign-in is link-only: HR creates the account, the identity provider only proves who someone is. Accounts are never created from an external login, and password sign-in stays available as a break-glass path.

Infrastructure you can put in a review

The service runs on AWS and Neon (managed PostgreSQL), both of which maintain SOC 2 Type II attestations for their own operations. A data processing agreement is available on request, and we only process the fields the product needs: name, work email, and work-location declarations.

Questions

The things HR teams ask us first.

How do employees record where they worked?
They choose "working from home" or "working from the office" — from the daily email in one click, from the web app, or from the mobile app on iOS and Android. All three write the same declaration for that person and that date.
Which countries and thresholds are supported?
France and Belgium both sit at 34 remote days a year, and Germany at 183. Thresholds are configured per country in the admin console, so a change to the legal position is a setting rather than a code change. Luxembourg-based employees are tracked without a limit by default.
Where is our data stored, and is it GDPR compliant?
Personal data is stored on servers inside the European Union (Germany) and processed under GDPR. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest, and we only process what the product needs: name, work email, and work-location declarations. A data processing agreement is available on request.
What happens when an auditor or a tax office asks?
You export the recorded history for the period they name. Each declaration carries its date and the person who made it, and every later change carries the actor, the reason and the previous value — so the record shows not just the final count but how it was reached. Data is retained for the statutory period.
Does signing in with our directory create accounts?
No. Accounts are provisioned by HR — from the admin console or a CSV import — and the identity provider only proves who someone is. Single sign-on is link-only: on first sign-in an existing account is matched by email and permanently bound to that directory identity. Anyone without an account is refused, and you can turn email matching off entirely to require an administrator to link each person.
Can HR correct a record after the fact?
Yes, and deliberately so. Employees raise a change request, HR approves or rejects it, and a direct override requires a written reason. Either way the original value and the correction are both preserved in the audit trail.
What if someone simply forgets to declare?
The dashboard shows exactly who has not declared, and a reminder is one click away. Reminders are sent automatically on your schedule and skip weekends and public holidays. Because undeclared days are visible rather than silently missing, gaps do not accumulate unnoticed.
How long does it take to get running?
Importing people and setting thresholds is an afternoon's work. Most of the elapsed time is the internal decision about your own remote-work policy, not configuration. A 14-day trial runs on your own data, with no card required.
